Heritage Travel: How to Trace Your Italian Roots

By Marco Nigro, Founder of Volta Travel | March 5, 2026 | 10 min read

There are approximately 80 million people of Italian descent living outside Italy. In the United States alone, over 17 million Americans identify as Italian-American. Many of them carry a last name, a recipe, and a story passed down through generations — but have never visited the village where it all began.

Heritage travel — the act of returning to your ancestral homeland — is one of the fastest-growing segments in luxury travel. And for good reason: it's not a vacation. It's a homecoming.

Why Heritage Travel Is Different

A regular trip to Italy is about seeing beautiful places. A heritage trip is about feeling something you can't explain — standing in a church where your great-grandparents were married, walking streets they walked, eating food that tastes like your grandmother's kitchen but more.

I've organized dozens of heritage journeys, and I can tell you: people who take these trips describe them as the most meaningful travel experience of their lives. I've seen grown men cry in tiny municipal offices when they hold their great-grandfather's birth certificate. I've watched families embrace distant cousins they didn't know existed, communicating through tears and hand gestures because they don't share a language.

This is not tourism. This is something deeper.

Step 1: Research Before You Travel

Start with what you know

  • Full names of ancestors who emigrated (including maiden names)
  • Approximate years of emigration
  • Any known cities or regions of origin
  • Ship manifests (available on Ellis Island records, Ancestry.com)
  • Naturalization papers (often list the exact comune of birth)
  • Old letters, photos, documents in family storage

    • Italian records you can access remotely

  • Antenati Portal (antenati.cultura.gov.it) — Digitized Italian civil records from 1800s-early 1900s. Free. Searchable by comune.
  • FamilySearch.org — Extensive Italian parish and civil records. Free.
  • Ancestry.com — Italian immigration and census records.

    • Key documents to find

  • Atto di nascita (birth certificate) of your ancestor
  • Atto di matrimonio (marriage certificate)
  • Ship manifest with their Italian comune listed

    • Once you have the specific comune (municipality), you have your destination.

    Step 2: Contact the Comune

    Before your trip, write to the comune's Ufficio Anagrafe (civil registry office). In Italian:

    > Gentile Ufficio Anagrafe,

    > Mi chiamo [your name] e sono un discendente di [ancestor name], nato/a a [comune] circa [year]. Vorrei richiedere una copia dell'atto di nascita per motivi di ricerca genealogica familiare. Sarò in visita a [comune] il [date] e mi piacerebbe visitare l'ufficio di persona.

    > Cordiali saluti, [your name]

    Many small comuni are incredibly helpful. Some will prepare documents in advance of your visit. A few will even connect you with local historians or potential relatives.

    Step 3: Plan the Journey

    The ideal heritage itinerary structure

    Days 1-3: Cultural gateway city

    Start in Rome, Naples, or Palermo — depending on your family's region. Decompress from jet lag, experience the broader Italian culture, and build anticipation.

    Days 4-7: Ancestral region

    Travel to your family's region. Stay in or near the ancestral village. This is the emotional core of the trip:

  • Visit the comune for records and documents
  • Walk the streets your ancestors walked
  • Visit the church where they were baptized/married
  • Find the family home (if it still exists)
  • Meet with local genealogists or historians
  • Potential meeting with distant relatives
    • Days 8-10: Reflection and celebration

    End with a few days of celebration — a cooking class featuring regional recipes, a wine tasting, a final dinner that honors both what you've discovered and the journey your family made.

    Budget

    Heritage trips tend to be longer and higher-value than standard Italy trips:

  • Duration: 10-14 nights (typical)
  • Budget: $6,000-12,000 per person
  • Why more expensive: Genealogical research, translators, private guides, document retrieval fees, longer stays

    • Step 4: The On-Ground Experience

    The comune visit

    This is usually the emotional highlight. You'll visit the Ufficio Anagrafe, where an officer will pull out massive leather-bound registry books — some dating back to the 1700s. Seeing your ancestor's name, handwritten in ink by a clerk 150 years ago, is a moment you'll never forget.

    Pro tip: Bring a good camera or phone. Photograph every document. The officer may allow you to see multiple records — births, deaths, marriages of extended family members.

    Finding the family home

    In small villages, this is often possible. Ask at the local bar (the social hub of every Italian village). Say your family name. In villages of 500-2,000 people, someone will know someone who knows something.

    Meeting relatives

    This happens more often than you'd think. In small Italian villages, surnames cluster. If your last name is the same as 30 families in the village, there's a good chance some of them are your distant cousins. Again, the local bar is your starting point. Or ask the comune officer — they often know who's related to whom.

    The language barrier

    If you don't speak Italian (and especially if the village speaks dialect), consider hiring a local guide/translator for the key days. The conversations you'll have — with the mayor, with a potential cousin, with the old woman who remembers a family with your name — are the most valuable part of the trip. Don't let language prevent them.

    The Emotional Preparation

    I always tell heritage travelers: prepare for emotion. You may feel:

  • Connection — to a place you've never been but somehow recognize
  • Grief — for ancestors who left everything behind for a better life
  • Gratitude — for the courage that made your existence possible
  • Belonging — a feeling of being "home" in a place you've never visited

    • These feelings are normal, profound, and exactly why heritage travel matters.

    The Italea Program

    The Italian government launched Italea as part of the "Tourism of the Roots" initiative, specifically targeting the 80 million people of Italian descent worldwide. The program offers:

  • Assistance with genealogical research
  • Connections with Italian comuni
  • Cultural events and heritage experiences
  • Support for obtaining Italian citizenship (if eligible)

    • This government backing has made heritage travel more accessible than ever.

    Italian Citizenship by Descent (Jure Sanguinis)

    Many Italian-Americans are eligible for Italian (and therefore EU) citizenship through descent. The requirements:

  • An ancestor who was an Italian citizen at the time of their child's birth
  • An unbroken chain of citizenship (no one in the chain renounced Italian citizenship before the next generation was born)
  • Documentation of the full lineage

    • This process can take 1-3 years but results in an EU passport — opening the door to living, working, and traveling freely in all 27 EU countries.

    While we don't handle citizenship applications directly, a heritage journey is the perfect opportunity to gather the required documents from Italian comuni.
