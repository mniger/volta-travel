<p class="text-sm italic text-charcoal/50 mb-6">By Marco Nigro, Founder of Volta Travel | March 25, 2026 | 8 min read</p>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">Every year, 65 million tourists visit Italy. Most of them go to the same three cities: Rome, Florence, Venice. They wait in line for hours, eat overpriced pasta near landmarks, and go home thinking they've "done Italy."</p>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">They haven't.</p>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">The real Italy — the one that steals your heart and changes how you see the world — exists in the spaces between the postcard destinations. In medieval villages where time moves differently. In hills where the only sound is wind through olive groves. In coastal towns where fishermen still pull up nets at dawn.</p>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">After planning 200+ bespoke trips across Italy, these are the 10 destinations I recommend to travelers who want something extraordinary.</p>

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">1. Orta San Giulio, Piemonte</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">Lake Como gets all the attention. Lake Orta, 90 minutes west, gets none — and that's exactly its magic. The medieval village wraps around a peninsular piazza overlooking Isola San Giulio, a tiny island with a 4th-century basilica. There are no tour buses. No selfie sticks. Just cobblestone alleys, a handful of trattorias, and the kind of stillness that makes you reconsider your entire life.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Couples seeking romance without crowds
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> April-June, September-October
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 2-3 nights

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">2. Matera, Basilicata</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">Once called "the shame of Italy" — a cave city so impoverished that the government evacuated it in the 1950s — Matera has been reborn as one of Europe's most extraordinary destinations. The Sassi (cave dwellings) have been transformed into boutique hotels, restaurants, and galleries. Walking through the city feels like walking through a living archaeological site spanning 9,000 years.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> History lovers, photographers, architecture enthusiasts
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> Year-round (magical in winter mist)
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 2-3 nights

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">3. Castelluccio di Norcia, Umbria</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">Between late May and early July, the high plains around this tiny village (population: 120) burst into what locals call "La Fiorita" — millions of wildflowers transforming the Sibillini Mountains into a technicolor carpet. It's Italy's answer to Provence, but without the crowds and with better food. The village was damaged by a 2016 earthquake but is rebuilding, and its lentils are the best in the world.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Nature lovers, photographers, spring/early summer travelers
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> Late May-early July for the bloom; year-round for hiking
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 2 nights (base yourself in Norcia)

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">4. Furore, Campania</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">On the Amalfi Coast but invisible from the main road, Furore is a "village that doesn't exist" — houses built into a dramatic sea fjord, connected by paths and staircases carved into rock. While millions visit Positano and Amalfi, Furore receives a fraction of visitors. The swimming cove at the bottom of the fjord is one of the most beautiful in the Mediterranean.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Adventurous travelers, Amalfi Coast visitors seeking seclusion
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> May-October
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 2 nights (or as a day trip from Amalfi)

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">5. Bergamo Alta, Lombardia</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">Milan's quieter neighbor has a medieval upper city (Città Alta) that is genuinely one of the most beautiful urban spaces in Italy. Reached by funicular, Bergamo Alta is enclosed by 16th-century Venetian walls (UNESCO World Heritage) and filled with Romanesque churches, hidden piazzas, and some of the best restaurants in Northern Italy. It was also the epicenter of Italian COVID suffering, which gave this already soulful city an added layer of resilience and emotion.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Architecture lovers, foodies, travelers who want culture without tourist infrastructure
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> Year-round
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 2-3 nights

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">6. Tropea, Calabria</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">Calabria is Italy's final frontier for tourism. Tropea, perched on a cliff above turquoise water, is its crown jewel. The famous red onions (cipolla rossa di Tropea) are sweet enough to eat raw. The beaches rival Sardinia. The seafood is the freshest you'll find anywhere in Italy. And the prices are half of what you'd pay on the Amalfi Coast.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Beach lovers, food enthusiasts, budget-conscious luxury seekers
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> June-September
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 3-5 nights

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">7. Civita di Bagnoregio, Lazio</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">Known as "the dying city," this Etruscan hilltop village is accessible only by a footbridge spanning a dramatic canyon. The soft rock it sits on is slowly eroding, making the village both heartbreakingly beautiful and philosophically profound — you're visiting a place that is literally disappearing. About 10 permanent residents remain.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Photographers, romantics, philosophy enthusiasts
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> Year-round (foggy mornings in autumn are surreal)
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> Day trip from Orvieto (1 hour)

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">8. The Langhe, Piemonte</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">Rolling hills, Barolo vineyards, truffle forests, and medieval towers. The Langhe region is Italy's answer to Burgundy — but with better food. This is where slow food was born. Where white truffles cost more than gold. Where every hilltop village has a castle and every valley has a family winery that's been producing wine for centuries.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Wine lovers, foodies, slow travelers
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> October-November (truffle season), May-June (spring beauty)
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 4-5 nights

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">9. Procida, Campania</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">While tourists fight for space on Capri, Procida — Italy's 2022 Capital of Culture — remains delightfully authentic. Pastel-colored fishermen's houses stack above a working harbor. The beaches are uncrowded. The restaurants serve whatever was caught that morning. At 4 km², it's small enough to walk everywhere but rich enough to fill three days.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Photographers, couples, travelers who love color and authenticity
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> May-September
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 2-3 nights

<h2 class="mt-10 mb-4 font-serif text-2xl text-charcoal">10. Val di Noto, Sicilia</h2>

<p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-charcoal/70 mb-4">After a devastating earthquake in 1693, eight towns in southeastern Sicily were rebuilt in extravagant Baroque style. Today, they're UNESCO World Heritage sites that rival anything in mainland Italy — but with a fraction of the visitors. Noto, Modica (famous for its chocolate), Ragusa Ibla, and Scicli are all within 30 minutes of each other, creating a perfect road-trip loop.</p>

<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Best for:</strong> Architecture lovers, chocolate lovers, road-trippers
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">When:</strong> April-June, September-November
<strong class="font-medium text-charcoal">Stay:</strong> 4-5 nights (base in Noto or Ragusa)
