# 7 Italian Food Mistakes Every Tourist Makes (And What to Do Instead)

*By Marco Nigro, Founder of Volta Travel | March 10, 2026 | 5 min read*

After 200+ trips to Italy, I've seen every food crime a tourist can commit. Here are the 7 most common mistakes — and what to do instead.

## 1. Ordering Cappuccino After 11am

**The mistake:** Ordering a cappuccino at lunch, after dinner, or really any time after mid-morning.

**Why it matters:** Italians consider milk-based coffee drinks a morning thing. Ordering cappuccino after a meal will mark you as a tourist and genuinely confuse your waiter.

**Do this instead:** After 11am, order "un caffè" (espresso), "caffè macchiato" (espresso with a splash of milk), or "caffè lungo" (longer espresso). After dinner, "un caffè" is the only correct answer.

## 2. Looking for "Fettuccine Alfredo"

**The mistake:** Searching restaurant menus for Alfredo sauce, chicken parmesan, spaghetti and meatballs, or garlic bread.

**Why it matters:** These dishes were invented by Italian-Americans. They don't exist in Italy. Asking for them is like asking a Japanese restaurant in Tokyo for a California roll.

**Do this instead:** Order what's actually on the menu. Ask the waiter "Cosa mi consiglia?" (What do you recommend?). Try regional specialties: cacio e pepe in Rome, trofie al pesto in Liguria, orecchiette in Puglia.

## 3. Eating Near Tourist Attractions

**The mistake:** Having lunch at the restaurant directly facing the Colosseum, the Duomo, or Piazza San Marco.

**Why it matters:** These restaurants pay enormous rent for their location. They make money from volume, not quality. The food is mediocre, the prices are inflated, and they know you'll never come back.

**Do this instead:** Walk 3-5 blocks away from any major attraction. Look for places where Italians are eating. Handwritten menus (not laminated with photos) are always a good sign. If there's a nonna visible in the kitchen, sit down immediately.

## 4. Putting Parmesan on Seafood Pasta

**The mistake:** Asking for Parmigiano on your spaghetti alle vongole, linguine ai frutti di mare, or any seafood dish.

**Why it matters:** In Italian cuisine, cheese and seafood don't mix. This is a deeply held culinary principle. Asking for parmesan on fish pasta will physically pain your waiter.

**Do this instead:** Seafood pasta is perfect as served. If you want cheese on pasta, order a meat or vegetable-based sauce.

## 5. Not Understanding "Coperto"

**The mistake:** Seeing a €2-3 "coperto" (cover charge) on your bill and thinking you've been scammed.

**Why it matters:** Coperto is a legitimate, legal charge that covers bread, table setting, and service. It's printed on every menu (by law). It is NOT a tip — it's a fixed per-person charge.

**Do this instead:** Expect coperto (€1.50-3.00 per person), accept it gracefully, and don't tip on top of it unless the service was exceptional. If "servizio incluso" appears on the bill, tip is already included.

## 6. Eating Dinner at 6pm

**The mistake:** Showing up at a restaurant at 6pm expecting to eat dinner.

**Why it matters:** Most Italian restaurants don't open for dinner until 7:30pm at the earliest. Romans eat at 8:30-9:00pm. Showing up at 6pm means you'll eat alone in an empty restaurant — if it's even open.

**Do this instead:** Have an aperitivo (drinks + snacks) at 7pm. Sit down for dinner at 8:00-8:30pm (earlier in the north, later in the south). This also means your lunch should be later — 1:00-2:00pm is normal.

## 7. Rushing Through Meals

**The mistake:** Eating quickly, asking for the check immediately, and treating the meal as fuel rather than an experience.

**Why it matters:** In Italy, a meal is a social event. The waiter will NEVER bring the check until you ask for it — because it's considered rude to rush you out. An Italian lunch lasts 1-2 hours. Dinner can be 2-3 hours.

**Do this instead:** Slow down. Order antipasto, then primo (pasta), then secondo (meat/fish), then dolce (dessert), then caffè. You don't have to order everything, but take your time with whatever you choose. When ready, say "Il conto, per favore" (the check, please).

The single best food advice for Italy: look at who's eating in the restaurant. If it's all tourists, leave. If it's Italians arguing passionately over their pasta, sit down.
